Affordable, local-language 3D anatomy for students — market, competitors and positioning

A Flutter app for iPad + Web (WebAssembly), with offline download on iPad and a phone companion for short-note review. Two audiences on one 3D engine: healthcare students/professionals and artists. Strategy: cheap, high volume, many languages, many countries.

The opportunity in one line. Market leaders charge $75–$100 per year (Complete Anatomy, Easy Anatomy) or sell only to institutions (Primal, and BioDigital since it retired its paid individual plan on 1 May 2026), and none speak the languages of South-East Asia. Meanwhile a cheap, offline, one-time-purchase app (Anatomy Learning, $16.99) shows the volume model works: third-party estimates put it at ~100K downloads and ~$100K revenue a month, led by Brazil. 1 2 3 4

Recommendation estimate

  • Position: "the 3D anatomy app students can afford, in their own language, that works offline", plus an Artist edition on the same 3D engine (écorché, posing, lighting).
  • Beachhead: Thailand (home advantage, zero Thai-language competition), then Vietnam, Indonesia and the Philippines. Use Japan and Brazil/LatAm as phase-2 expansion. Enter the US/EU only through the artist niche.
  • Price: about 1/5 of the leaders. Around $14.99/yr or a $19.99 one-time "edition" globally, with country-adjusted prices down to ~$3–6 in SEA and India using Apple's 900 price points (from $0.29). Apple
  • Moat: localisation pipeline (terminology DB in Latin + English + local language), national-exam-aligned quizzes, and partnerships with faculties. Big Western players are unlikely to follow on price, because cutting it would cannibalise their $75–$100 users.
  • Key risk: 3D content quality and medical accuracy cost more than the app. Flutter 3D on the web is still maturing: Flutter Scene is pre-1.0, and Safari Wasm needs opt-in on Safari 26.2+. 1 2

Killer features by group

Premium medical Complete Anatomy · Visible Body · Easy Anatomy

  • Huge validated model libraries: 700+ to 11,000+ structures
  • Virtual dissection and AR
  • Video lectures, clinical videos, radiology (CT/MRI) correlation
  • Courses, quizzes, flashcards; Easy Anatomy now has an AI assistant

Win on depth and brand. Lose on price ($75–$100/yr) and language coverage. 1 2 3

Exam-prep / web Kenhub · BioDigital · Primal

  • Exam-style quizzes and spaced repetition (Kenhub has 490+ quizzes)
  • Web-embeddable 3D and LMS use (BioDigital)
  • Institutional library licences (Primal)

Win on the institutional channel. BioDigital left the paid individual market in May 2026. 1 2

Value medical Anatomy Learning · Catfish · Asian locals

  • One-time or low price ($17–$30)
  • Works offline
  • Asian locals add culturally specific content, e.g. acupuncture and meridians (掌上3D解剖, ~$20/yr), and MRI-based motion (teamLab)

Proves the volume model. Weak brand and design. 1 2 3

Artist Art Anatomy · Skelly · Manikin · 3D Anatomy for the Artist

  • Posing (manual, or from a photo in Art Anatomy)
  • Studio lighting, rim light, clay or outline render
  • Écorché layers from skin to bone
  • Export cut-outs for drawing over

The field is fragmented: no single app does posing, écorché, lighting and form simplification well. Prices are $7–$30. 1 2 3 4

Gaps we can own

1. Language gap (strongest)

Complete Anatomy lists 6 languages, Visible Body 7, BioDigital 8, Easy Anatomy 8 and Anatomy Learning 10. None of them include Thai, Vietnamese or Indonesian. In our search, the only anatomy-adjacent apps with Thai are artist tools (Manikin, Art Anatomy). table search

2. Price gap

Students outside the US/EU face $75–$100/yr, or a first-year discount that disappears at renewal. Asian local players price at about $20/yr. 1 2

3. Individual-buyer gap

BioDigital retired its paid individual plans on 1 May 2026, and Primal sells to institutions. Individual students are underserved. 1

4. Medical + art crossover

No player serves both audiences. A shared 3D asset pipeline lets us ship two SKUs for roughly the cost of one estimate.

5. Web + offline + phone companion

The leaders are native apps of 1.3–1.5 GB. A Wasm web version reaches Android-heavy markets (India, Indonesia, Vietnam) before we build native Android, and a phone companion app for short notes and flashcards fits commuting students estimate.

6. National-exam alignment

Quizzes mapped to each country's licensing and university anatomy syllabus are hard for global players to localise at our price estimate.

Segment priority estimate

  1. Pre-clinical medical, nursing and allied-health students (year 1–2). Largest volume, they must learn anatomy, and they are price-sensitive. This is our core.
  2. Art students and hobby illustrators on iPad. Low CAC via creator content, global reach, little need for medical review, and fast to ship.
  3. Faculties and institutions (B2B2C bulk seats). Stabilises revenue and acts as a distribution channel.
  4. Practising professionals (physio, fitness, nursing CPD). Later, through a patient-education add-on.

Positioning

For healthcare and art students who need to understand the body in 3D, [Product] is the anatomy app they can afford, in their own language, that works offline on iPad and in any browser. Unlike Complete Anatomy or Visible Body, it costs about a fifth as much, speaks Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian and more, and has an Artist edition built on the same accurate 3D body.

Medical edition

  • Systems explorer, layers and isolate; offline packs per system
  • Terminology in Latin, English and the local language side by side
  • Quizzes aligned to each country's exams; spaced repetition on the phone companion
  • Short notes and flashcards synced to the phone
  • Teacher mode with shareable scenes for lectures

Artist edition

  • Écorché layers from skin to bone, with muscle names
  • Posing with anatomical joint limits, plus a pose library
  • Studio lighting, clay, outline and planar ("form simplification") render
  • Transparent-PNG export to draw over in Procreate
  • Proportion guides (heads-tall, Loomis-compatible)

Go-to-market plan by market recommendation

🇹🇭 Phase 1 — Thailand (months 0–9)

  • Ship the iPad MVP in Thai and English: musculoskeletal system first, plus the Artist edition.
  • Run an ambassador programme in medical, nursing and physio student clubs and art faculties.
  • Get 2–3 anatomy lecturers as content advisers, and pilot faculty licences.
  • Short Thai anatomy content on TikTok, YouTube and Facebook (e.g. "1 muscle in 60 s").
  • Time launches to the academic calendar: back-to-school and pre-exam promos.

🇻🇳🇮🇩🇵🇭 Phase 2 — SEA (months 6–18)

  • Add Vietnamese and Indonesian. The Philippines can launch in English straight away, with a nursing focus.
  • Make web (Wasm) a first-class channel, because these markets are Android-heavy.
  • Partner with local study-content creators and nursing-review centres.
  • Set aggressive local price tiers.

🇯🇵🇧🇷 Phase 3 — Japan, Brazil/LatAm (months 12–24)

  • Japan: lead with the Artist edition (manga and illustration schools); medical second, against teamLab.
  • Brazil/LatAm: Portuguese and Spanish, competing on the proven one-time-purchase model.
  • Ship the Android native app in this phase.

🇮🇳🇺🇸 Opportunistic

  • India: web-first, very low price, and institutional deals. Huge volume but low ARPU (see the revenue model).
  • US/EU: Artist edition only, via App Store search and art YouTubers. Do not fight Elsevier head-on in medicine.
  • China: deprioritise, because of local players and payment and regulatory friction.
